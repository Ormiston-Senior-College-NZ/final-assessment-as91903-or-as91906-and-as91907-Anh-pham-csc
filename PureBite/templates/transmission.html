1. From home

- Many cases of food poisoning originate in home kitchens due to poor hygiene practices, improper cooking, and inadequate food storage. It can be caused by cross-contamination, which can occur when raw foods come into contact with cooked foods, and undercooked meats, eggs, and seafood are common culprits.

- Some common ways food poisoning can happen at home can be devided into 5 main parts:

+ Improperly preparing meals: Cooking at incorrect temperature or not following proper cooking guidelines (for example: chicken is safe to eat at 74°C (165°F), but the cooker only cooks it at 65°C (149°F) and serves it straightaway, the chicken is not well-cooked, the person who eats this might get food poisoning.)



+ Handling raw meat and eggs: Not washing hands after handling raw meat and eggs can transfer harmful bacteria to food.

+ Not refrigerating food properly: Food left out at room temperature for too long can become contaminated. (for example: you left a bowl of rice outside for the whole night without anything covering)

+ Using contaminated water: Drinking water that has been contaminated with harmful bacteria or viruses can also lead to food poisoning. (for example: water that is polluted from waste or contains chemical compounds)

+ Cross-contamination: Using the same cutting board for raw meat and vegetables can spread bacteria from one to the other.

2. From external factors

- Restaurants and Food Services: Restaurants are significant sources of food poisoning, especially when high volumes of food are prepared. Issues can arise from staff hygiene, improper food storage, and temperature control. Sick employees handling food can lead to widespread contamination.

- Food Processing and Distribution: Contamination can occur at any point in the food supply chain, from farming and harvesting to processing and distribution. Foods can be contaminated by bacteria, viruses, and parasites during these stages, especially if proper sanitation practices are not followed.

- For instance, the recent outbreak at Taiko Ponsonby, a Japanese restaurant in Auckland, has been a significant concern due to the number of reported cases and the severity of symptoms experienced by affected individuals. The restaurant has cooperated with investigations and is working to implement corrective actions as advised by health authorities.

- In addition to EpiSurv records, a single outbreak of suspected scombroid fish poisoning (two notified cases) relating to smoked fish bought from a supermarket (Table 28) was referred to NZFS. Leftover fish samples were sent to ESR for testing and histamine levels of concern were confirmed, providing strong evidence for scombroid fish poisoning. Onsite investigation occurred and control failures were resolved. - Contaminated food: Certain foods are more likely to cause food poisoning, including raw or undercooked meats, eggs, unpasteurized dairy products, and raw fruits and vegetables that have not been properly washed. - Some other sources of contaminated foods are: colorful mushrooms, pufferfish, raw bamboo shoots,...

3. Bacteria

- Salmonella: The most common sources of Salmonella infection are: water, milk and dairy products, shellfish harvested from contaminated river outlets containing sewage and farm effluent.

- Staphylococci: The common sources of Staphylococci are human nose, mouth, skin, hands, spots, boils, septic cuts, etc. The common food vehicles are dairy products, cold cooked meat and poultry, peeled cooked prawns...

- Clostridia: These bacteria occur mainly in soil, and food poisoning is caused usually by contaminated of vegetables. Many pigs, as well as rats and mice, habour these bacteria.
