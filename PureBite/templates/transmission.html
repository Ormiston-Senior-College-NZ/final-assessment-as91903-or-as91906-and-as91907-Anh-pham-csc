1. From home

- Many cases of food poisoning originate in home kitchens due to poor hygiene practices, improper cooking, and inadequate food storage. It can be caused by cross-contamination, which can occur when raw foods come into contact with cooked foods, and undercooked meats, eggs, and seafood are common culprits.

- Some common ways food poisoning can happen at home can be devided into 5 main parts:

+ Improperly preparing meals: Cooking at incorrect temperature or not following proper cooking guidelines (for example: chicken is safe to eat at 74°C (165°F), but the cooker only cooks it at 65°C (149°F) and serves it straightaway, the chicken is not well-cooked, the person who eats this might get food poisoning.)

2. What is the food poisoning situation in New Zealand and in the world?

- Food poisoning is a significant public health issue in New Zealand, affecting around 200,000 people annually, with 500 cases reported each day. => Ratio 1/1000

According to 2023 health data on the seven most common notifiable diseases, food poisoning disproportionately affected children under 4 and adults over 70. For five of these seven diseases, both age groups recorded rates exceeding 75%.

- New Zealand has reported a rise in most major foodborne infections in 2022, according to recently published data. Most 27 outbreaks, with 253 cases in 2022, were linked to commercial food operators, and only five were associated with food prepared in consumer homes.

- An estimated 600 million - almost 1 in 10 people in the world - fall ill after eating contaminated food and 420 000 die every year. US$ 110 billion is lost each year in productivity and medical expenses resulting from unsafe food in low- and middle-income countries.
